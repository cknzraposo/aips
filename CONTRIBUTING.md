# NZ AI Policy Sandbox - Collaborator Brief

*Working title: NZ AI Economy Simulator*  
*Date: 2026-04-17*  
*Shareable draft*

---

## One-line description

**A transparent NZ sector-calibrated policy sandbox for testing AI policy tradeoffs under uncertainty.**

---

## What this project is trying to do

This project is building a paper-grade framework and scenario model for thinking about AI policy in New Zealand.

The starting point is simple: NZ's current AI baseline is methodologically messy. The most-cited **44%** figure is not business adoption at all, but a trust measure. Other numbers in circulation range from **32% to 87%**, depending on source, sample, and definition.

That means much of the public conversation around "NZ AI adoption" is flatter and more confident than the evidence supports.

We want to replace that with a more honest framework: a **sector-calibrated, whole-economy policy sandbox** that compares different policy structures under uncertainty.

---

## Core research question

Does aggregate AI policy, designed around a single national average, systematically produce worse outcomes than sector-targeted policy in New Zealand?

A related question sits underneath it:

> When is it better to support direct adoption in sectors, and when is it better to invest in enabling capacity such as technology, skills, procurement, infrastructure, and diffusion?

---

## Why the project matters

New Zealand is already making decisions about:
- AI capability
- public sector adoption
- sector investment
- regulation
- digital health
- skills
- procurement
- industry support

But the policy conversation often behaves as though a single national adoption number is enough.

It isn't.

Different sectors face different constraints:
- healthcare has governance and equity constraints
- public service faces capability, culture, and scale barriers
- finance is shaped by regulation and stability concerns
- manufacturing has a clearer productivity case
- construction is fragmented and low-maturity
- technology acts as an enabling sector for the rest of the economy

If those differences matter, then policy designed around a single average will misallocate effort.

---

## Project framing

This is **not** a forecasting engine.

It is a **policy sandbox** designed to compare scenarios transparently under uncertainty.

The emphasis is on:
- structured comparison
- visible assumptions
- evidence classes
- clear caveats
- useful policy reasoning

Not on producing a false sense of quantitative certainty.

---

## Scope: whole economy, tiered model

The model now uses a tiered whole-economy structure.

### Tier 1 - full explanatory sectors (9)
These sectors carry the richest policy narratives and will be modelled in greatest depth:
- Agriculture
- Manufacturing
- Professional Services
- Public Sector
- Technology
- Healthcare
- Construction
- Financial Services
- Retail and Wholesale

### Tier 2 - simplified sectors (6)
These sectors matter for whole-economy coverage and policy realism, but will use lighter parameterisation:
- Education and Training
- Transport, Postal and Warehousing
- Accommodation and Food Services
- Administrative and Support Services
- Information Media and Telecommunications
- Utilities

### Tier 3 - residual sectors (4)
These sectors close the whole-economy denominator:
- Mining
- Rental, Hiring and Real Estate Services
- Arts and Recreation Services
- Other Services

This matters because a 9-sector-only model is not enough for an honest economy-wide aggregate-policy comparison.

---

## Scenarios to compare

### 1. Aggregate policy
Broad support distributed across the economy.

### 2. Targeted demand-side policy
Support focused on sectors with acute bottlenecks or large unlockable gains.

### 3. Targeted supply-side policy
Support focused on enabling capacity such as technology, skills, infrastructure, procurement, and diffusion.

---

## What we are trying to avoid

We are explicitly trying to avoid:
- a flashy simulator with weak methods
- false precision
- economy-wide claims built on partial denominator coverage
- black-box modelling logic
- treating AI adoption as a single national number

---

## What success looks like

A successful first version would:
1. correct the broken baseline
2. model the economy with denominator honesty
3. compare policy structures transparently
4. show where sector structure changes outcomes
5. survive serious criticism from economists and sector experts

---

## Why collaborate now

Because the project is at the right stage for criticism.

The framing is now clearer than the original concept, but the model architecture and final parameterisation are still open. That makes this the useful moment to challenge assumptions before complexity hardens into sunk cost.

---

## Current working claim

**New Zealand's current AI baseline is too incoherent for single-average policy design. A sector-calibrated, whole-economy policy sandbox can reveal why targeted policy may outperform broad aggregate approaches, and where it may not.**

# NZ AI Adoption Baseline - Source Comparison

*Why the numbers do not agree*
*Compiled: 2026-04-14, reframed 2026-04-18*

---

## Summary

There is no single credible NZ AI adoption rate.

Figures in circulation range from **32% to 87%**. They measure different things, sample different populations, use different definitions of "adoption," and carry different selection biases. Some are not adoption measures at all.

No two sources agree because no two sources are measuring the same thing.

This is the baseline problem: policy decisions about AI investment are being made against a methodologically incoherent evidence base.

---

## The figures in circulation

| Source | Year | Figure | What it actually measures | Population | Methodology |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| NZIER / Spark QSBO | 2024 | **32%** | SMEs with any plans to evaluate/invest in AI | Nationally representative panel | Structured survey |
| KPMG / University of Queensland | 2023 | **44%** | Public trust - believe benefits of AI outweigh risks | 17,000+ individuals across 17 countries | Global survey, NZ subset |
| Datacom State of AI | 2023 | **48%** | Larger businesses "using some form of AI" | 200 senior leaders | Convenience sample |
| Datacom State of AI | 2024 | **67%** | Same as 2023, one year later | 200 senior leaders | Convenience sample |
| AI Forum | Sep 2024 | **~67%** | Respondents using AI in organisations | Self-selected network | Opt-in survey |
| AI Forum | Mar 2025 | **82%** | Same methodology | Self-selected network | Opt-in survey |
| AI Forum | Aug 2025 | **87%** | Same methodology | Self-selected network | Opt-in survey |

### Notes on the KPMG figure

The 44% is frequently cited as if it were an adoption rate. It is not. It measures public sentiment about whether AI benefits outweigh risks.

The original study is KPMG and University of Queensland, *Trust in Artificial Intelligence: A Global Study*, 2023. A PDF of the global report is available at:
`https://kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/xx/pdf/2023/09/trust-in-ai-global-study-2023.pdf`

The study was later expanded (2024-2025 waves, reportedly covering up to 47 countries with University of Melbourne involvement), but the expanded NZ-specific data has not been independently verified for this project. Until we can confirm the exact figures from a primary source, we cite the 2023 study and note the expansion.

### Notes on the Datacom figure

Datacom's 2024 report also uses a "44%" figure, but for a different question entirely: "44% say NZ is well placed to adapt to the AI revolution and keep pace globally." This is a readiness perception measure, not adoption. The adoption figure from the same report is 67%.

These two unrelated "44%" figures from different sources measuring different things illustrate the problem. Numbers circulate without provenance, and conflation is easy.

---

## The incoherence

The 2.7x gap between NZIER/Spark (32%) and AI Forum (87%) reflects:
- **Different populations:** nationally representative SME panel vs self-selected AI-interested network
- **Different definitions:** "plans to evaluate" vs "using in organisation"
- **Different selection bias:** representative vs convenience/opt-in

Neither produces a reliable national adoption rate.

**MBIE's NZ AI Strategy (July 2025)** cites both Datacom's 67% and NZIER/Spark's 68%-not-adopting figure in the same document without reconciling them.

---

## What this means for the sandbox

The sandbox does not depend on any single adoption figure being correct or incorrect.

It calibrates per-sector adoption from the best available data for each sector, documents the source and confidence level for every input, and tests policy scenarios against the range of plausible baselines.

The contribution is not "this number is wrong." The contribution is: the numbers are incoherent, policy built on any single one is fragile, and a sector-calibrated framework provides a more honest basis for comparing policy options under that uncertainty.

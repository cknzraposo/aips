# New Research — Analysis Notes (18 April 2026)

## 1. AI Forum NZ — Full Productivity Report (September 2024, 34 pages)

This is the **full version** of the report we previously analysed from a summary extraction. Key new data:

### Headline findings (232 valid responses)
- **67% adoption** (155 of 232 organisations)
- Generative AI most common type (55% of AI users)
- **96% reported AI made workers more efficient**
- **Only 8% reported AI replacing employees** (5-10% displacement where it occurred)
- **50% reported positive financial impact on output**
- **62% reported operational cost savings** (mostly $1,001-$50,000 range)
- **52% reported initial setup costs**; 62% ongoing costs
- **49% reported AI created new career opportunities**
- **29% reported less need to hire** (sinking lid effect)
- Top 5 use cases: Marketing (16%), Administration (13.8%), Design (9.6%), Project Management (9.4%), Software Development (9%)

### Methodology (critical for our project)
- **232 valid survey responses** from AI Forum network
- **Self-selected sample** — not nationally representative
- Lead researcher: Dr Andrew Lensen (VUW) — acknowledges selection bias
- Survey period: mid-2024
- Some industries had fewer than 5 respondents (excluded from industry breakdown)
- "Per area" question structure deterred many from completing → methodology being revised
- **Key quote from Lensen:** "with a third of NZ firms yet to adopt AI, a more proactive approach is needed"

### Case studies with quantified impacts
1. **Classic Group (construction):** Purchase order pricing reduced from 2 hours to 15 seconds, 98% accuracy. AWS Bedrock + Claude Sonnet.
2. **Aware Group (AI consultancy):** Statement of work creation from 5 hours to sub-10 minutes. Engineering reports from 30-40 hours to 20 minutes. Animal health testing from days to 12 seconds with 93% accuracy (up from 47% off-the-shelf).
3. **University of Otago:** Personalised course recommendation system using GPT-4.

### Simulator implications
- Confirms the **self-selection bias** we identified in provenance analysis
- The 67% figure is the AI Forum's September 2024 reading (we had this)
- **New detail:** by-function breakdown (marketing/admin/design dominate — not domain-core AI)
- The efficiency vs displacement data is useful for labour pressure modelling
- Case studies provide concrete NZ evidence for C7 fields
- **Strong correlation between AI spend and financial benefit** — useful for investment-return modelling

## 2. Tech New Zealand — Tech Innovation Manifesto 2026 (55 pages)

### Key data points for S05 (Technology sector)
- **$24 billion GDP contribution (8% of economy)** — updated from $23.8B
- **119,520 jobs in tech sector**
- **$11.4 billion tech exports (11% of NZ total)**
- **$1.2 billion invested in R&D in ICT/tech** (29% of NZ's total R&D)
- **24,012 companies in the tech sector**
- **31% of Year 13 students studying tech**
- **2,555 Māori tech graduates (7%)**
- **98,290 ICT jobs across the economy** (including IT workers in non-tech firms)
- **Tech exports growing 5x faster than the economy**
- By 2045: projected **250,000+ labour shortfall** without policy reform

### Policy context (critical for D fields)
- **Four foundations:** digital infrastructure, clean energy, investment & talent, exports & global growth
- **Agentic commerce:** calls for urgent legal framework for AI agents buying/selling
- **Quantum:** national strategy recommended now
- **SaaS in government:** outdated procurement rules identified as barrier
- **Gene editing:** separate regulatory pathway recommended (distinct from GMO)
- **Digital identity:** DIST Framework Act 2023 passed, now needs adoption investment
- **Cybercrime:** $1.6B lost annually
- **R&D incentives:** calls to uplift to globally competitive levels
- **NZ Super Fund:** review mandate to direct late-stage capital into NZ tech firms
- **Double taxation:** review rules to attract international firms
- **Fees-free policy:** realign to growth sectors (tech, biotech, fintech, agritech)
- **Game development subsidy:** retain as sector approaches $1B exports

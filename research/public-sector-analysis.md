## Key Findings

### 1. NZ's Strategic Approach to AI (Cabinet Paper, June 2024)

**Cabinet agreed to five domains:**
1. **Strategic approach:** light-touch, proportionate, risk-based regulation. No standalone AI Act. Leverage existing frameworks (Privacy Act, Human Rights Act, Copyright Act, etc).
2. **Public service AI:** GCDO to lead system-wide AI adoption. Agencies encouraged to adopt AI while managing risks.
3. **Economy-wide harnessing:** MBIE to develop cross-portfolio AI work programme. AI Roadmap for private sector.
4. **International engagement:** MFAT to prioritise international AI initiatives. OECD AI Principles adopted as NZ's key direction.
5. **National security:** DPMC coordinating emerging/critical/sensitive technology work programme.

**Critical policy signals for the simulator:**
- **"Businesses are slow to adopt AI, due in part to uncertainty about the future regulatory environment"** — regulatory uncertainty is an explicit adoption barrier
- **"Other countries are actively seeking to harness AI's economic potential. We must take action"** — NZ is behind peers
- **Light-touch regulation preferred** — no standalone AI Act. This is a distinct policy posture vs EU (comprehensive) and China (state-driven)
- **Cross-Party AI Caucus (CPAIC)** exists and supports government AI use — bipartisan support

### 2. Public Service AI Framework (January 2025)

One-page framework with **five principles** (adapted from OECD):
1. **Inclusive, sustainable development** — innovation, efficiency, resilience, address inequalities
2. **Human-centred values** — privacy, democracy, human rights, human oversight
3. **Transparency and explainability** — awareness, explainable outcomes
4. **Safety and security** — safety by design, data protection, traceable data, risk management
5. **Accountability** — governance, regulatory frameworks, auditing, capability growth

**Six-pillar work programme:**
1. Governance
2. Guardrails
3. Capability
4. Innovation
5. Social licence
6. Global voice

### 3. Barriers to Public Service AI Uptake (Nov 2024 briefing to Minister)

The most operationally relevant document. Three barriers identified by GCDO:

| Barrier | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Capability** | Lack of AI capability and understanding across the public service is a limiting factor. Individuals can't use tools; organisations don't understand deployment potential. |
| **Culture and perceptions** | Early GCDO guidance emphasised risk management and practices to avoid — likely had a **"chilling effect"** on agencies' uptake. Risk aversion is the dominant culture. |
| **Scale** | Pilots have not demonstrated clear productivity dividends to justify further investment. Agencies hesitant to invest without confidence in ROI. |

**Key quotes:**
- "Many agencies are exploring AI... though these are mostly pilot cases and have been conducted at a small scale"
- "Positive developments in relatively small pockets of the public service"
- "Early guidance from GCDO had a greater emphasis on risk management... which has likely had a chilling effect"
- "NZ is not ranked highly in regard to AI readiness, lagging behind many of our international comparators (e.g. Australia)"
- Commission met with **Microsoft NZ and Asia region** representatives — observations aligned with GCDO findings

**Proposed solutions:**
- Central learning platform via Leadership Development Centre
- Positive messaging from Ministers to shift culture from risk-aversion
- Centralised approach for common use cases (cross-agency pilots, central investment)
- Fiscal incentives for AI investment (retain and reinvest savings)
- AI uptake targets for chief executives, monitored by GCDO
- Cloud-first approach to digital infrastructure (foundation for AI)

### 4. Legislation Landscape (from Accessible Version)

NZ has **no AI-specific legislation**. Existing frameworks that apply:
- Bill of Rights Act 1990
- Human Rights Act 1993
- Privacy Act 2020
- Public Records Act 2005
- Public Service Act 2020
- Copyright Act 1994
- Official Information Act 1982
- Algorithm Charter for Aotearoa NZ (2020) — signed by 21+ agencies
- Te Tiriti o Waitangi obligations

Cabinet approach: **light-touch, proportionate, risk-based**. Further intervention only to unlock innovation or address acute risks.

## Implications for Simulator — Public Sector Archetype ("Employment Story")

### This changes the archetype narrative

The earlier framing was "lowest adoption but accelerating fastest" based on GCDO's 272 AI use cases. These documents reveal a more nuanced picture:

1. **Adoption is mostly pilots, not scaled deployment.** "Small pockets" not system-wide transformation. The 272 use cases / 55 operational figure from GCDO 2025 needs to be read against this context — many use cases may be small-scale experiments.

2. **The "chilling effect" is real and acknowledged.** Early GCDO guidance was too risk-focused, creating institutional hesitancy. This is now being corrected but the culture shift takes time.

3. **Scale is the binding constraint.** Pilots don't generate enough ROI to justify further investment. Without centralised coordination and funding, agencies won't move beyond pilots.

4. **Capability gap is acute.** Both individual AI literacy and organisational deployment capability are lacking.

### For policy scenarios
- **Aggregate scenario:** Public sector absorbs GDP-share of investment but structural barriers (capability, culture, scale) mean returns are slow — validates the "employment story" archetype
- **Demand-targeted scenario:** Directing investment specifically to capability building, centralised tools, and culture change in public sector could unlock the largest employer in NZ
- **Supply-targeted scenario:** Public sector's cloud-first approach and Microsoft engagement means it benefits indirectly from tech sector investment
